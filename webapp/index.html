ABOUT O-RAP

A research tool for
understanding orbit estimates.

O-RAP means Orbital Reliability Assessment and Prediction. It is a reproducible research project studying whether information in Two-Line Element records can help describe when successive SGP4 position estimates disagree.

01 / THE RESEARCH QUESTION

When do consecutive orbit estimates disagree?

Satellite operators publish orbital estimates as TLE records. O-RAP examines consecutive, distinct TLE epochs for a satellite. It propagates the older TLE to the newer TLE’s epoch, then compares that position with the position from the newer TLE propagated to the same epoch. This produces a consistent comparison time for each pair.

Older TLE→SGP4 at shared epoch↔Newer TLE
02 / WHAT THE LABEL MEANS

A study definition

A pair is labelled unreliable when the two calculated TEME positions differ by more than 1 km; differences at or below 1 km are labelled reliable. This is a comparison between two TLE-based estimates, not ground-truth orbit error.

03 / WHAT THE MODEL SEES

13 orbital features

The orbital-only XGBoost model uses the source TLE’s mean motion, eccentricity, inclination, node, pericenter, anomaly, drag term, motion derivatives, semimajor axis, period, apoapsis, and periapsis. The interval to the next record is excluded because it is not known at the source TLE time.

04 / DATA AND EVALUATION

Time-ordered research

The repository contains records for 14 satellites and 242,547 constructed consecutive pairs. The chronological evaluation separates earlier training data from later validation and test periods. It also includes leave-one-satellite-out experiments to examine performance on satellites held out during training.

05 / CALIBRATION

Readable probabilities

Platt scaling is fitted using validation predictions to make the orbital-only model’s probabilities better aligned with observed study labels. The classification cutoff is also selected on validation data; it is a research operating point, not a universal boundary.

06 / LIMITATIONS

What O-RAP does not claim

The project does not compare predictions with independently measured satellite positions. Its labels are based on disagreement between two SGP4 propagations using successive TLEs. Estimates should not be interpreted as physical orbit-error measurements, collision probabilities, conjunction screening, or operational safety advice. Performance can vary across satellites, time periods, and decision thresholds.

07 / REPRODUCIBLE PROJECT

Research artifacts remain part of the work

O-RAP includes raw GP history data, processed pairs, validation and audit outputs, feature and split files, trained estimators, calibration outputs, evaluation tables, and publication figures. The website presents those results without retraining or changing the scientific pipeline.
